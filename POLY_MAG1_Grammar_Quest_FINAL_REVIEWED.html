Lesson 1

🏫 Simple Sentences

Education

🧠 꼭 기억해요

A simple sentence tells one complete idea.
It needs a subject (who or what) and a predicate (does what).
Begin with a capital letter and end with punctuation.
🎵 WHO + DOES WHAT = A COMPLETE SENTENCE!

🔊 소리 내어 읽기

Students learn.
Our teacher reads a book.

Lesson 2

⚖️ Kinds of Sentences

The Court System

🧠 꼭 기억해요

A statement tells something and ends with a period.
A question asks something and ends with a question mark.
A command tells someone to do something.
An exclamation shows strong feeling and ends with an exclamation mark.
🎵 TELL . ASK ? COMMAND . FEEL !

🔊 소리 내어 읽기

The judge listens.
Did you hear the rule?
Please sit down.
What a fair decision!

Lesson 3

🤝 Compound Sentences

Volunteers

🧠 꼭 기억해요

A compound sentence joins two complete sentences.
Use a comma before and, but, or, so.
Both sides must be able to stand alone.
🎵 COMPLETE IDEA, COMMA, JOINING WORD, COMPLETE IDEA!

🔊 소리 내어 읽기

We picked up trash, and we planted flowers.
I wanted to help, but I was late.

Lesson 4

🤖 Common & Proper Nouns

Engineering

🧠 꼭 기억해요

A common noun names any person, place, animal, or thing.
A proper noun names a special person, place, day, month, or title.
Proper nouns begin with capital letters.
🎵 SPECIAL NAME? CAPITAL GAME!

🔊 소리 내어 읽기

Lesson 5

⚽ Plural Nouns

Sports

🧠 꼭 기억해요

Most nouns add -s.
Add -es after s, x, z, ch, sh.
Consonant + y: change y to i and add -es.
Some f/fe words change to -ves.
🎵 S, ES, IES, VES — LOOK AT THE END!

🔊 소리 내어 읽기

ball → balls
box → boxes
city → cities
knife → knives

Lesson 6

🎨 Verb Tenses

Visual Arts

🧠 꼭 기억해요

Present tense tells what happens now or regularly.
Past tense tells what already happened.
Future tense often uses will + base verb.
Circle time words before choosing the verb.
🎵 EVERY DAY = PRESENT, YESTERDAY = PAST, TOMORROW = WILL!

🔊 소리 내어 읽기

I paint every day.
I painted yesterday.
I will paint tomorrow.

Lesson 7

🎎 Using Commas

Traditions

🧠 꼭 기억해요

Use commas for three or more items.
Use a comma after Yes, No, Well.
Use commas in dates and between a city and country.
Use a comma before a conjunction in a compound sentence.
🎵 LIST, YES/NO, DATE, TWO SENTENCES — PAUSE WITH A COMMA!

🔊 소리 내어 읽기

We ate rice, soup, and fruit.
Yes, I know the dance.
August 15, 2026

Lesson 8

🎭 Abstract Nouns

Performance Arts

🧠 꼭 기억해요

A concrete noun can be seen, heard, touched, tasted, or smelled.
An abstract noun names an idea, feeling, quality, or state.
Many abstract nouns end in -ness, -ment, -tion, or -ity.
🎵 CAN I TOUCH IT? YES = CONCRETE, NO = ABSTRACT!

🔊 소리 내어 읽기

dancer = concrete
joy = abstract
kindness = abstract
excitement = abstract

Lesson 9

💡 Writing Quotations

Inventions

🧠 꼭 기억해요

Quotation marks show the exact words a person says.
Begin the quotation with a capital letter.
Put end punctuation inside the quotation marks.
Use a comma to separate the speaker tag from the quotation.
🎵 SPOKEN WORDS INSIDE QUOTES — PUNCTUATION STAYS INSIDE!

🔊 소리 내어 읽기

Lina said, “This robot can move.”
“Can it talk?” asked Ben.

🧪 Irregular Plural Nouns

Inventions

🧠 꼭 기억해요

Learn them as word pairs.
🎵 IRREGULAR MEANS: DON'T JUST ADD S!

person → people

Quick Review

⚡ 작년 문법 한눈에 보기

전체 규칙을 짧게 확인하고 10문제를 풀어요.

Sentences & Capitalization

A sentence has a complete idea. Capitalize the first word and proper names.

Past Verbs

walk→walked, play→played, go→went, see→saw, eat→ate

Pronouns

I, you, he, she, it, we, they replace nouns.

Prepositions

in, on, under, beside, between, behind

Adjectives

Describe nouns: a bright star, three small birds.

Adverbs

Tell when or how: yesterday, soon, quietly, carefully.

Possessive Nouns

One owner: Mia’s book. Plural owners: students’ desks.

Special Pronouns

myself, yourself, himself, herself, ourselves, themselves

Future Tense

will + base verb: We will travel.

Comparatives

tall→taller→tallest; beautiful→more beautiful→most beautiful
